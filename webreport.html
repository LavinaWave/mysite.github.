Web report

I really hope I’ll be able to post the project on time!

About cliens:
It was not uncommon for history exam preparation sites to post recommendations for the best films on their social networks. My target audience is people who want to start preparing for history Olympiads and are looking for courses for this. As a “bait” for such people, a website with films is created to encourage the customer not just to sign up for courses, but also to receive a historical analysis of the films they have decided to watch. Of course, it is assumed that the course has its own page on various social media platforms, from which interested parties can follow the link.

The project has a fairly simple plan: a main page and 4 subpages that can be accessed via the timeline. I won’t dwell on this — I’m desperately short on time!

The most difficult part of this entire process was creating decent popups and a timeline! The timeline took the longest, and in the end, I asked the AI to calculate the timeline trajectory for me. Actually, as you can easily notice, I have quite a lot of JavaScript code — I’ve already programmed in many languages (C, C++, Python, Java) before, so initially I wanted to add a script to change the design — and then it came in handy for pop‑ups (and since I have access to Google, I was simultaneously googling what and how to do there).

The website won’t work on a phone — not only because it’s designed for comfortable movie viewing on a computer, but also because I didn’t have enough time to properly adapt everything (for the same reason, I don’t have a link to the map — sorry!).

The audio was selected for each page depending on the year and copyright. I tried to provide a source for each image, and I thanked the artist who created the cat button in the night theme, as he allowed the use of his works for a non‑commercial website. And my website is essentially non‑commercial, since its functionality is completely free!

In terms of animation, I have animated images on each page, as well as a timeline. The main page features archival footage of Petrograd in the 1920s. This same page also features archival footage from the 1980s in the Soviet Union. They were initially chosen as placeholders, but in the end I got used to them and decided to keep them for the ambiance. The fact that Gorbachev ended up on this page was purely accidental.

During this course, I learned how to create timelines! And websites! As for the downsides, I’d like to mention the presentations — they usually serve as a brief summary of the lesson, so you can absorb the basic material from the slides, and to clarify any unclear points, you can refer to the recording (after all, if you missed a lesson, listening to a 3‑hour recording is very time‑consuming). In the course, however, the slides intimidated me… and quite a bit at that. Because of this, I had to absorb most of the material on my own.

I checked all my codes for compliance with the standards — they seem to meet the requirements, but I don’t have time to attach every screenshot.

How long did all of this take, about 15 hours? I’ve been actively working on the website for the past week, and in the end, that time wasn’t enough — for the last two days, I’ve been doing nothing but work on the website. If I had more time, the movies would be more varied!

The idea of having a film catalog was probably a bit questionable, but I just really love watching historical films.

It was tested in two different browsers.
